General

A bit about me

I got into software around 2020 and ended up in the ERP world soon after. Since then most of my working hours have gone into Dynamics NAV and Business Central: reading C/AL that someone wrote in 2009, rebuilding it as AL extensions, and checking that the numbers still add up after go-live.

The part I like most is the edge of the ERP. Business Central is a very good ledger. It's less good at talking to a POS terminal in an airport, or to a .NET service that wants new orders every five minutes. That's where C# and ASP.NET Core come in, and where I spend a lot of my time.

Most of my clients are retail and duty-free businesses in the Gulf and the Maldives. I work remotely from Kathmandu, which is a couple of hours ahead of the Gulf, so I'm usually well into my day by the time they log in.

Questions

Things people ask me

What does a Business Central technical consultant actually do?

The technical half of a Business Central project: AL extensions, integrations, data migration, reports, upgrades from Dynamics NAV and performance fixes. The functional consultant works out what the business needs, and I make the system do it without breaking the next update.

Can you upgrade our Dynamics NAV to Business Central?

Yes. I've worked on upgrades from NAV 2009 R2 and NAV 2015 to modern Business Central, including turning C/AL modifications into AL extensions, upgrading data and migrating reports. My upgrade guide covers how I approach it.

Can Business Central talk to our .NET application?

Yes. I build ASP.NET Core APIs, background workers and Azure Functions that use Business Central's standard and custom APIs with OAuth 2.0 service-to-service authentication.

Do you work with clients outside Nepal?

Most of my clients are outside Nepal. I've delivered Business Central work for retail and duty-free businesses in Bahrain, the Maldives and the wider Gulf, all remotely from Kathmandu.

Which versions do you work with?

Business Central online and on-premises, plus Dynamics NAV from 2009 R2 onwards when there's an upgrade or an old system to keep alive.
